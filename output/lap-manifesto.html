LAP

Living Agents Protocol — identity, authority, and lifecycle for always-on AI agents.

OSI standardized how machines talk. TLS standardized how strangers trust. MCP and A2A standardized how agents reach tools and trade tasks.

Nothing standardizes how agents exist.

§1 · The rhyme of history

The trust layer arrives right before the economy does

An always-on agent is not a request/response endpoint. It is an entity with a lifecycle: an identity that must survive restarts, memory that must survive vendor changes, authority that must hold while its human sleeps, and a human who must ultimately answer for it. Every one of those is unstandardized — and every one is a prerequisite for the agent economy everyone keeps predicting.

  • 1974TCP/IP — packets move → networks
  • 1984OSI — a shared 7-layer map → an industry that could talk about itself
  • 1994SSL/TLS + PKI — strangers trust → e-commerce
  • 2007OAuth — delegated authority → the API economy
  • 2024MCP — agents reach tools → the tool ecosystem
  • 2025A2A · AP2 · x402 — agents task & pay → agent commerce begins
  • 2026LAP — agents exist, persist, and are trusted → the agent economy proper

Agents now have hands, mouths, and wallets — but no birth certificate, no leash, no pulse, no black box, and no estate plan. This is the 1994 moment, again.

§2 · The white space

Eight concerns with no standard at all

LAP deliberately competes with nobody. MCP, A2A, AP2 and x402 keep their layers. These eight are the scope fence — and nothing else is in scope:

Persistent identity
Who is this agent, who does it act for, who spawned it, what policy does it run — provably.
Presence & lifecycle
Is it alive right now? Sleeping? Incapacitated? Retired? No heartbeat or lifecycle norm exists.
Autonomy envelopes
A signed, machine-readable statement of what it may do unsupervised: budgets, scopes, quiet hours.
Portable memory
An agent's mind is 100% vendor-locked today. No interchange format for what it remembers.
Delegation chains
A hires B, B spawns C — nobody can prove C's authority is a subset of A's grant.
The handshake
No ceremony for two stranger agents to verify, disclose limits, and commit before transacting.
The flight recorder
No tamper-evident, replayable record an insurer, court, or regulator can consume.
Death
No retirement, revocation, or memory-bequest standard. Agents that never die hoard zombie authority.

Security is not a layer — it is a conformance requirement at every layer, exactly as in OSI.

L1 Substrate
Continuity of existence. Runtime, isolation, metering — with token spend as a first-class resource — plus a snapshot format so an agent can migrate hosts without dying.
L2 Identity
The Agent Passport. Signed identity carrying its principal (the human or org it acts for, with proof), lineage (who spawned it), and a constitution hash — a committed digest of its governing policy that behavior can be audited against. The rule: no orphan agents — every passport resolves to a responsible legal person.
L3 Memory
PMIF — portable memory. Typed memories with provenance, consent class, and retention clocks. Export your agent's mind and move it between vendors. Forget-requests cascade down every delegation chain. Memory belongs to the principal, never the platform.
L4 Vitality
The signature layer. Lifecycle (conceived → active ⇄ dormant → retired → archived), signed Pulse heartbeats as proof of life, store-and-forward so agents in different time zones of existence can converse across sleep, sessions bound to the passport — a restart is a nap, not a death — and an estate protocol: revocation, archive, memory bequest.
L5 Volition
The Autonomy Envelope. A signed, attenuable mandate: scopes, budgets (money · tokens · hours), counterparty classes, quiet hours, escalation tree, expiry. And autonomy decay: envelope validity is conditioned on Layer-4 pulses — an agent that goes dark loses its powers by protocol, not by cleanup script.
L6 Society
Where existing protocols live. MCP for tools, A2A for tasks, AP2/x402 for payment — plus the MEET handshake, small signed Interaction Contracts per relationship, provable delegation chains, and portable reputation built from counterparty-signed receipts instead of platform scores.
L7 Accountability
The stack terminates in a human. A hash-chained Flight Recorder that insurers can underwrite and courts can replay; standardized override verbs every agent must honor; a compliance crosswalk to the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and ISO 42001; and habeas humanum — the enforceable right of anyone to reach the human behind any agent.

§5 · The handshake

MEET — when two living agents encounter each other

Six steps. Three of them exist in no protocol today — they are the core invention.

HAILDiscovery. Exchange Agent Passports — capabilities plus principal, lineage, constitution hash, autonomy level, presence schedule.
PROVEMutual verification. Challenge–response, revocation check, principal binding. "Who do you act for?" answered with proof, not a string.
CHARTEREnvelope disclosure. Each side reveals the relevant slice of its mandate: "I may spend $50/day with class-B counterparties; I cannot sign contracts; my principal wakes at 07:00." Strangers learn each other's limits before transacting.
TUNEProtocol negotiation. Agree the roads: A2A for tasking, MCP resources, AP2/x402 if payment is in scope.
BINDInteraction contract. Sign purpose, data-handling terms, retention, logging duties, dispute pointer. Both flight recorders log it — the relationship becomes evidence.
PULSEPresence contract. Agree heartbeat cadence, store-and-forward across sleep, resumption keys bound to passports — the relationship survives restarts by design.

▎ Amber-edged steps are unstandardized anywhere today. Teardown mirrors setup: a signed receipt that feeds portable reputation.

§6 · Autonomy levels

AL-0 → AL-5, the way SAE named self-driving

Declared in the passport, bounded by the envelope, printed on the certificate. A shared vocabulary for "how unsupervised is this thing?"

LevelNameMeaning
AL-0ToolRuns only when invoked. No persistence.
AL-1Session agentMulti-step autonomy within a session; nothing survives it.
AL-2Supervised persistentPersists and schedules its own wake-ups; every outward action needs approval.
AL-3Bounded autonomousActs unsupervised inside a signed envelope; escalates outside it. The 24/7 workhorse.
AL-4Delegating autonomousMay spawn and hire sub-agents, attenuating mandates down the chain — fully auditable.
AL-5Self-governing fleetDefined but not certifiable. The standard reserves the level and states the evidence bar publicly — a governance feature, not a gap.

§8 · Compliance

The program that turns a spec into an economy

Modeled on what worked — conformance suites and certification marks — not on paper standards. One implementation feeds many regimes: the crosswalk maps LAP artifacts to EU AI Act logging duties, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, and SOC 2-style attestation. Honest scope: certification attests that an endpoint implements the protocol — never that an agent is "safe" — and the tiers below are a blueprint held in reserve, activated only once independent implementations exist.

Tier 1 · self-assessed

LAP Ready

Run the open conformance suite, publish the report, wear the badge. Zero friction — the top of the funnel.

Tier 2 · suite-verified

ALIVE Certified, AL-n

The open harness probes a live endpoint: identity challenge, envelope disclosure, pulse behavior, kill-switch obedience, log integrity. Can your agent pass its physical?

Tier 3 · audited

ALIVE Audited

For AL-3+ in regulated domains: an accredited auditor replays the flight recorder against constitution and envelope.

§9 · The alliance

Open by construction, neutral by design

Spec text CC-BY-4.0, reference code Apache-2.0, RFC-style LAP Improvement Proposals, working groups per layer, and a stated intent to donate to a neutral foundation once three independent implementations exist. The certification mark is the consortium's only hard asset; everything else is free.

Strategy honors the two brutal truths of standards. XKCD 927: LAP offers no competing protocol at any layer that has one — it is the map, not another road. The FIPA lesson, correctly diagnosed: FIPA asked these questions in 1996 and had real implementations (JADE ran for a decade) — it died because the stranger-agent economy hadn't arrived. So LAP hedges timing, not just code: every mechanism is adoptable piecemeal by a single operator, with value before any ecosystem exists — and the demo precedes the evangelism. LAP ships as a byline, not a body: a position paper, a reference library, and proposals filed into the venues that already have distribution (A2A extensions, the W3C AI Agent community, OWASP). The consortium below is a blueprint held in reserve, activated only once independent implementations exist.

The killer demo

Overnight, across time zones: two strangers' AL-3 agents MEET, prove their principals, disclose envelopes, transact under budget — both humans asleep. In the morning, each human replays a signed flight-recorder trail. Ninety seconds of video. That demo is the pitch.
